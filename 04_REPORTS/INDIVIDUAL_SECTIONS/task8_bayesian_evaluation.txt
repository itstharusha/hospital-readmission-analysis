TASK 8: EVALUATION OF BAYESIAN STATISTICAL METHODS
Hospital Readmission Consultancy

1. NAIVE BAYES

Naive Bayes applies Bayes' theorem while assuming conditional independence of predictors within each outcome class. It is fast and useful as a baseline, but the independence assumption is doubtful here because age, diagnoses, medication burden, length of stay, and prior utilisation are related. It should be used only as a comparative baseline, not assumed to be superior to logistic regression.

2. BAYESIAN LOGISTIC REGRESSION

Bayesian logistic regression assigns prior distributions to coefficients and combines them with the observed data to obtain posterior distributions. It can report a posterior mean, credible interval, and the probability that an effect is positive or negative.

Potential benefits:

- Incorporates defensible prior clinical knowledge.
- Quantifies uncertainty in coefficients and patient-level predictions.
- Supports hierarchical effects when data from multiple hospitals are available.
- Provides a natural probabilistic framework for risk thresholds and resource allocation.

Limitations:

- Prior specification requires clinical and statistical justification.
- Results should be checked under reasonable alternative priors.
- MCMC models require convergence, effective-sample-size, and posterior-predictive checks.
- A Bayesian model is not automatically better calibrated or more accurate.
- Deployment requires technical expertise and a governance process.

3. BAYESIAN DECISION MAKING

Prediction and action are different problems. A hospital can use a cost matrix to choose a threshold. If missing a patient who will be readmitted is substantially more costly than an unnecessary follow-up, a threshold below 0.50 may be reasonable. The threshold must be estimated from local costs, intervention capacity, patient preferences, and clinical safety requirements rather than asserted in advance.

Expected loss can be expressed as:

Expected loss(action) = sum over outcomes of P(outcome | data) times cost(action, outcome).

The action with the lowest expected loss is selected. This framework makes the trade-off between false negatives and false positives explicit.

4. RECOMMENDED HYBRID APPROACH

For initial implementation, retain interpretable logistic regression as the operational baseline and evaluate a Bayesian logistic model as a validation and decision-support layer. A future multi-hospital model could use hospital-specific intercepts with shared predictor effects. Priors should be weakly informative unless external evidence strongly supports a more specific prior.

Report posterior predictive discrimination, calibration, subgroup performance, credible intervals, and sensitivity to prior choices. Display uncertainty to clinicians and preserve clinical override authority.

5. CONCLUSION

Naive Bayes is suitable as a baseline. Bayesian logistic regression is valuable for uncertainty quantification, multi-hospital modelling, and cost-sensitive decisions, but it should complement rather than replace a transparent operational model until it has been validated locally.
